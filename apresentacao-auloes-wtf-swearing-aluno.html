Etapa 6 · Careful Who You Say That To
Reaction vs.
Insult.
Some expressions are aimed at the moment. Others are aimed at a person. That changes everything.
"F*** you!"
Direct insult
Strong direct insult. Aggressive regardless of context. Not softened by familiarity.
PT: "Vai se f***" / "Vai à merda"
"F*** off!"
Aggressive / Playful
Can mean "go away" aggressively — but between close friends, it can be used playfully (like "no way!"). Context is key.
PT: "Vai embora" / "Para com isso" (jocoso)
"Bitch"
Offensive / Reclaimed
Offensive term, often with gender connotations. In some groups it has been reclaimed as a term of endearment or empowerment — but it remains risky outside those contexts.
PT: "Vadia" / "Cadela" (pejorativo)
"A**hole"
Insult — character
Insult for someone selfish, unpleasant or inconsiderate. Strong, but less gendered than "bitch".
PT: "Idiota" / "Babaca" / "Canalha"

⚠️ Familiarity doesn't guarantee acceptance. Even between close friends, a directed insult can hurt.

Etapa 7 · Would You Say That?
Would you
say that?
Four situations. Which response fits best? (More than one can be right!)
Situation 1 — At work 💼
Your computer stops working during an important meeting.

✓ Safe: "Oh no!" — professional, appropriate anywhere.

⚠ Risky: "Damn!" — common but some workplaces don't accept it.

✗ Avoid: "This f***ing…" — very inappropriate in professional settings.

Situation 2 — With your best friend 👯
Your friend tells you they won a million dollars.

✓ Safe: "That's amazing!" — enthusiastic and always appropriate.

✓ Also works: "Holy shit!" — genuine surprise between friends. Very common.

⚠ Careful: "F*** you!" — sometimes used as "no way!" between very close friends, but risky and can be confusing.

Situation 3 — At a restaurant 🍽️
The waiter brings the wrong order.

✓ Best choice: Polite and effective in any situation.

⚠ Avoid: "What the hell?!" — rude and unnecessary in a service situation.

✗ Never: Insulting the waiter is always inappropriate and unkind.

Situation 4 — Watching a movie 🎬
A character does something completely unexpected.

✓ All can work — it depends on intensity and your company!

"No way!" — neutral surprise. "What the hell?!" — moderate. "What the f***?!" — very strong.

Watching alone or with friends: all acceptable. With family or strangers: stick to "No way!"

Etapa 9 · Safe or Strong?
Safe or
Strong?
Classify each expression. Click to see where it usually lands — and why.
"Oh my gosh!"
Generally safer
Very mild — acceptable in most contexts, including in front of children.
"Damn!"
Use with caution
Relatively mild, but some environments (school, formal work) still consider it inappropriate.
"What the heck?!"
Generally safer
Euphemism for "what the hell" — milder and more TV-friendly, but still informal.
"What the f***?!"
Strong or offensive
Very strong and vulgar. Avoid in public, professional, or family settings.
"This sucks."
Use with caution
"Sucks" is informal and can be impolite, but is widely used and generally not offensive.
"He's an a**hole."
Strong or offensive
Direct insult targeting someone's character. Always offensive when directed at someone.
Etapa 10 · Real English Upgrade
Say it with
more feeling.
Neutral English is still real English. But sometimes you want more expressiveness. Click to see options — then choose yours!
"This movie is very good."
✅ "This movie is amazing!"
⚡ "This movie is so damn good!"
🔥 "This movie is f***ing amazing!"
"I'm very tired."
✅ "I'm exhausted."
⚡ "I'm tired as hell."
"I'm surprised."
✅ "No way!"
🔥 "Holy shit!"
"This situation is bad."
⚡ "This situation sucks."
🔥 "This is f***ing terrible."

💡 The neutral options are also real English. Choosing not to swear is not less fluent — it's contextually smarter in many situations.

Etapa 12 · Final Challenge
Do you get
the meaning?
Four idioms with "shit." What does each one actually mean?
"This party is the shit."
✅ B — "the shit" = something or someone excellent. The article "the" flips the meaning completely!
"I don't give a shit."
✅ B — Total indifference. "I don't give a damn" is a milder alternative.
"Get your shit together."
✅ C — "Get yourself organized / focused." Used when someone is falling apart or being unreliable.
"Shit happens."
✅ B — A very common phrase expressing acceptance. "These things happen" or "c'est la vie" — just more direct!
